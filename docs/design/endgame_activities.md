# The endgame's activities: a brainstorm (28 Oct)

Matt, starting a fresh full playthrough: "we're aiming for a minimum of 20 hours of content and then if it's still
lacking we'll need to refine the endgame further - which will likely come in the form of PoE's style of different
activities for different goals allowing the players choice in what to grind." A brainstorm only: nothing decided,
nothing built; what his playtest says about the hours decides whether any of it is wanted.

## What the endgame is today

One loop with steps: signals (papers decoded on the Radio into a site run at a level, Kev's story among them) push the
reach; the Dial spends what the reach earns; the three Orders (the Grid, the Archive, the Mint) are the pinnacles; the
Issue turns the money; the County's twelve places want issued kit; the rebuild, paragon and the collection log run
under it all. Everything pays in the same coin - a better runner - so there's one thing to grind, the reach.

## The idea: one activity a goal

PoE's league mechanics each answer a different "what do I want today?": currency, a specific item, crafting, XP,
a boss. Each below is a side door off the Map with its own loop and its own payout, so a player picks what to grind
by what they want. Each reuses what exists (the fight, the crates, the crew, the papers, the Fence, the Bench).

## How they'd hang together

- **One door each on the Map**, opening as the endgame does: the wagers with the Radio, the Pit and the auction with
  the Issue, the convoys and the checkpoint with the County, the allotments and debts as camp pieces.
- **Each pays one thing well and the rest poorly**, so choosing is the point: wagers for notes, the Pit for paragon,
  the convoys for a named piece, the allotments for crafts.
- **The reach still matters**: each activity's level follows it, so the runner's power is still the gate.
- **The bot** plays each through the same sim, so the balance reads them; an activity it can't play isn't read.

## My recommendation, if the hours fall short

1. **Kev's wagers** and **the Pit** first: small, they reuse nearly everything, and they add choice (what to bet, when
   to cash out) with almost no new screens.
2. Then **the convoys**: the story's already pointing there, and "this lorry carries the piece I want" is the most
   compelling goal on the list.
3. Then one crafting activity (**the allotments** or **debts**), picked by what the playtest says the Bench lacks.
4. The tunnels and the checkpoint last: the biggest, and the most likely to overlap with what the reach already is.
